# Pi-chart and the agent-native clinical record opportunity

## Executive assessment

**Bottom line:** yes, there is a real and defensible product direction here, but only if pi-chart stays disciplined about being a **clinical record substrate** rather than drifting into “yet another OSS EHR” or “generic healthcare agent framework.” The public repo already states the key thesis unusually clearly: the chart is canonical, current state is a query, derived summaries are disposable, and there is no separate memory system because the chart itself is the agent’s durable long-term memory. It also already implements a single claim/event envelope, append-only correction semantics, evidence-chain and open-loop views, validator-enforced invariants, and a roadmap that explicitly aims to prove value through a broad-but-shallow EHR skeleton rather than a full production EHR. That is a materially different starting point from classic OSS EHRs and from newer AI-enablement platforms. citeturn3view0turn3view1turn4view0turn4view1turn5view0turn6view0turn6view2turn6view3

The distinctive opportunity is not “AI for EHRs.” It is **a different record primitive**: instead of treating the chart as a mutable CRUD database and bolting on AI, summaries, audit logs, and compliance artifacts afterward, pi-chart can treat each charted fact as a time-bound, source-attributed, evidence-linked, append-oriented claim that is later projected into notes, handoff, review, task, and agent contexts. In the market and standards landscape I reviewed, traditional OSS EHRs such as Medplum, OpenMRS, GNU Health, and OpenEMR either remain FHIR/resource-centric or encounter/relational-model-centric, while AI-forward platforms such as HealthChain and Gobii mostly sit on top of existing records and workflows. The closest architectural relative is work in the entity["organization","openEHR Foundation","ehr specs organization"] ecosystem, where versioned compositions, contributions, and mandatory commit audits are core repository constructs, but even there the center of gravity is versioned clinical documents and archetyped structures, not an agent-native claim graph with first-class evidence/open-loop projection. citeturn8view0turn9view0turn9view2turn12view0turn12view2turn19view0turn22search0turn22search1turn25view0turn26view0turn29view0turn29view1turn35view2turn35view3

That means pi-chart’s opportunity is real, but narrow: it wins only if it becomes the **best provenance-native clinical memory layer** for humans and agents, with strong standards boundaries. If it instead tries to compete feature-for-feature with mature EHRs, practice-management platforms, or generic agent runtimes, it will lose the part that is actually differentiated. This conclusion is partly **direct evidence** from the repo and public docs, and partly **inference** from how other systems describe their own models and audit layers. citeturn4view1turn9view0turn25view0turn29view0turn31view0turn31view1turn35view0turn35view2

## What pi-chart already has

The current public design has more signal than most very-early healthcare repos because it already names a coherent substrate. Directly from the repo, pi-chart defines one clinical claim/event envelope, supports multiple storage carriers under that envelope, keeps `_derived/` non-authoritative, routes all writes through sanctioned APIs, and exposes six view primitives: `timeline`, `currentState`, `trend`, `evidenceChain`, `openLoops`, and `narrative`. It also enforces append-only correction patterns through `supersedes`/`corrects`, patient isolation, explicit source attribution, and structured validation. That is enough to say pi-chart is not merely “an AI wrapper over files”; it is already trying to be a provenance-aware chart kernel. citeturn3view0turn3view1turn5view0turn6view0turn6view1turn6view2turn6view3turn6view4

The roadmap and ADRs also show unusually good product discipline. The repo explicitly says the near-term goal is to prove pi-chart as **clinical memory** through a broad, shallow EHR skeleton across six surfaces: flowsheets/vitals, nursing assessment, notes/narrative charting, orders/meds/interventions, labs/diagnostics, and care plan/handoff. It also explicitly rejects becoming a full production EHR and states that FHIR is a **boundary adapter, never the internal model**. That combination matters: it means the team is already oriented toward the right proving ground for a substrate thesis while resisting the trap of premature “full EHR” ambition. citeturn4view0turn4view1

The strongest current weakness is also visible in the repo. Pi-chart has strong primitives and good internal vocabulary, but today it still looks more like a well-designed local substrate than a system that could reliably sit between real clinical systems, agents, and reviewers. The roadmap itself leaves FHIR as a future boundary adapter, defers UI, treats SQLite and vector indexing as later layers, and still has open seams around the pi-sim/pi-agent loop. In other words, the primitives are promising, but the interoperability and operational surface needed for external credibility is still ahead. That is not a flaw in the thesis; it is the next product problem. citeturn4view0turn4view1

## Feature recommendations for pi-chart

If pi-chart is framed as **agent-native EHR infrastructure**, the table stakes are no longer “it stores facts nicely.” The table stakes become: stable version semantics, external interoperability, actor/source/transform traceability, deterministic projection, reviewer-visible uncertainty and open loops, and minimally credible operational controls around audit, access, and replay. The good news is that pi-chart’s repo already points in that direction. The gap is execution and boundary surface, not thesis. citeturn4view0turn4view1turn31view0turn31view1turn32view3turn35view2turn35view3

### Must-have next

- **Finish the six-surface broad EHR skeleton around one coherent patient story.** The repo already names this as the immediate proof surface, and without it pi-chart remains an elegant substrate demo instead of a clinically legible memory system. The acceptance standard should be: one write can legitimately affect review, handoff, task/open-loop state, and downstream note projection. citeturn4view0turn4view1
- **Make actor, attestation, and review state first-class.** Pi-chart already captures `author` and `source.kind`, but credible clinical infrastructure also needs a durable distinction among agent suggestion, agent action, human verification, co-sign, supersession, and rejection. This is where pi-chart should borrow from FHIR `Provenance`, `AuditEvent`, and openEHR commit-audit concepts without becoming internally FHIR/openEHR-shaped. citeturn3view0turn5view0turn6view0turn31view0turn31view1turn35view2turn35view3
- **Ship a real boundary adapter layer soon.** The repo is right to keep FHIR out of the internal model, but it now needs outbound and inbound mappings for the core workflow resources: `Observation`, `Condition`, `Task`, `CarePlan`, `ServiceRequest`, `MedicationRequest`, `MedicationAdministration`, `Communication`, `DocumentReference`, `Provenance`, and `AuditEvent`. Without this, pi-chart risks looking philosophically interesting but operationally isolated. citeturn4view0turn12view0turn22search0turn22search6turn25view0turn31view0turn31view1
- **Add deterministic projection and replay exports.** “Show me what changed, why it mattered, what supports it, what remains open, and what the next shift should watch” should be a first-class exportable artifact, not just an internal notion. The repo already calls this a “memory proof projection”; make it a formal API/render target. citeturn4view0turn4view1turn6view2turn6view3
- **Harden external evidence references.** The existing evidence model is already unusually good for an early repo. The next step is reliable references to outside artifacts and transformations, including stable IDs for imported FHIR resources, documents, lab interfaces, monitor feeds, and agent-generated derivations so evidence chains do not stop at the repo boundary. citeturn6view1turn6view2turn6view4turn31view0

### Differentiating bet

- **Make “chart once, project many” the flagship capability.** This is the most valuable differentiator in the repo and in ADR 016: the same claim stream should project into notes, handoff, task state, clinician review panels, and agent context instead of being rewritten into each place. Most incumbent systems duplicate prose or attach AI outputs after the fact; pi-chart can make projection the main act. citeturn3view0turn4view1turn9view0turn22search1
- **Double down on evidence chains and open loops as first-class chart surfaces.** `evidenceChain` and `openLoops` are not just neat views; they are the clearest evidence that pi-chart is designing for accountability instead of note generation. Mature EHRs often have tasks, audits, and histories, but far fewer make “why does the system believe this?” and “what remains unresolved?” core user-facing chart primitives. citeturn3view1turn6view2turn6view3turn12view2turn22search1
- **Model uncertainty and contradiction directly.** The roadmap’s movement toward typed evidence refs, transform provenance, and contradiction semantics is exactly right. A defensible agent-native record should preserve not just the winning fact, but also contested or superseded reasoning and the evidence basis that produced it. That is a stronger safety posture than simply retaining old versions. citeturn4view0turn6view2turn31view0turn32view3turn35view2

### Standards and interoperability

- **Emit standard artifacts even if you never ingest or store them internally.** For buyers and integrators, the internal model matters less than whether pi-chart can responsibly interoperate. FHIR resources should be export/import adapters; `AuditEvent` and `Provenance` should be boundary artifacts; AI Transparency on FHIR should be watched and selectively adopted where it improves downstream observability. citeturn4view0turn31view0turn31view1turn30search2turn30search6
- **Study openEHR contribution semantics for batch commits.** Even if pi-chart never adopts openEHR templates internally, the idea that a coherent clinical transaction should commit with structured audit details is directly aligned with the repo’s “one commit per agent decision cycle” discipline. That is worth translating into explicit contribution/transaction metadata. citeturn3view0turn35view2turn35view3
- **Support history and as-of semantics rigorously.** FHIR history and openEHR versioning both reinforce the need for replayable, immutable timelines. Pi-chart already has `asOf`-aware views; it should make those semantics externally testable and interoperable. citeturn3view1turn4view0turn32view3turn35view2

### Compliance and audit foundation

- **Separate human review from machine generation everywhere.** The boundary between agent observation, inference, synthesis, action, and human attestation should be visible at the event level and queryable at the projection level. Medplum’s AI material is right that guardrails and auditable actions are mandatory; pi-chart should encode those distinctions deeper, at the chart primitive itself. citeturn3view0turn9view0turn9view5
- **Add read-path observability, not just write-path provenance.** Pi-chart already looks stronger on write-side integrity than many systems. To be credible for clinical-agent governance, it eventually also needs who-read-what/when and which workflow or agent context consumed which facts. That maps more closely to `AuditEvent` than `Provenance`. citeturn31view1turn32view0turn32view2
- **Support tamper-evident archival and legal-retention semantics.** openEHR’s indelibility and commit-audit stance is a good reference point. Even if pi-chart is not yet a regulated product, buyers will expect a story for logical deletion, redaction, signatures, retention, and exportable audit records. citeturn35view2turn35view3

### Defer

- **Do not build a full production EHR surface now.** The repo is correct to reject full-EHR sprawl. Scheduling, billing, generalized hospital operations, comprehensive CPOE, payer workflows, and broad multi-user administration are overreach at this stage. citeturn4view1turn21search4turn23search18
- **Do not make vector memory the primary story.** The roadmap is right to place vector indexing later. If pi-chart leads with embeddings or retrieval, it will collapse back into “AI context layer” language and dilute the stronger substrate thesis. citeturn4view0
- **Do not claim compliance-ready or EHR-replacement positioning.** Gobii’s healthcare page is careful to say “designed to support HIPAA compliance,” not that the platform itself solves healthcare compliance. Pi-chart should be equally disciplined. citeturn29view1
- **Do not overfit to one simulator or one demo slice.** ADR 016 is right that proving breadth of observable context matters more than polishing a narrow scenario. Keep that discipline. citeturn4view1

## Product framing

**Best concise positioning:**  
**“Pi-chart is an agent-native clinical record substrate: a provenance-rich, append-oriented clinical memory layer that projects into clinician and agent workflows.”**

That wording keeps the important claims and drops the ones that would create unnecessary skepticism. It fits the repo’s own thesis that the chart is durable memory, that FHIR is a boundary adapter rather than the internal model, and that the next proof is a broad clinical skeleton instead of a production EHR. citeturn3view0turn4view0turn4view1

**Phrases to avoid**
- “AI-native EHR.” It sounds like a complete EHR replacement and invites maturity/compliance objections that the repo explicitly does not claim. citeturn4view1
- “Clinical memory-context.” It is understandable, but slightly too narrow because it sounds like prompt retrieval or context-window tooling instead of a durable, reviewable record layer. Pi-chart is trying to be more than context. citeturn3view0turn4view1
- “Full audit/compliance solution.” The standards and comparators show that auditability is multilayered; pi-chart should say it is **provenance-native** and **audit-friendly**, not that it fully solves healthcare compliance by itself. citeturn29view1turn31view1turn35view2
- “Replace your EHR.” The better message is “sits beneath, beside, or between existing EHR workflows while making clinical memory reviewable for humans and agents.” That is much more believable against Medplum, OpenEMR, and OpenMRS. citeturn8view0turn12view4turn21search4

**Is “clinical memory-context” too narrow?**  
Slightly yes. It under-describes the durable record, evidence linkage, attestation, and workflow projection parts. It is better as an explanatory phrase than as the primary category label. citeturn3view0turn4view1

**Is “EHR” too broad?**  
Yes as a standalone label, at least today. The repo itself says the project should not become a full production EHR and that breadth matters only where it creates observable clinical context. Using “EHR” nakedly will pull the conversation toward billing, scheduling, certification, and replacement claims. citeturn4view1

**Better category language**
- **Agent-native clinical record substrate**
- **Provenance-native clinical memory layer**
- **Clinical memory and workflow substrate**
- **Clinical record kernel for humans and agents**

Of those, the first is the strongest because it preserves the distinctiveness of the idea without overclaiming scope. It also differentiates pi-chart from FHIR platforms, OSS EHRs, and agent runtimes that do not redefine the clinical record primitive. citeturn8view0turn25view0turn29view0turn35view0

## Open questions and limitations

Two scope notes are worth keeping in mind. First, I treated **HealthChain** as the currently active open-source healthchainai/dotimplement project because that is the clearest public OSS AI/FHIR integration framework matching your comparison set; there is also a separate commercial “Health Chain” payer-data platform with a different purpose. Second, for **Gobii**, the public evidence is strongest for a general self-hosted agent platform plus a healthcare solution page, not for a healthcare-specific clinical-record codebase. That does not invalidate the comparison; it just means Gobii is best treated as an **agent-operations comparator**, not a chart-substrate comparator. citeturn25view0turn26view1turn29view0turn29view1

The final judgment is therefore an inference, but a high-confidence one: **pi-chart has a defensible opportunity if it becomes the provenance-native clinical record layer that AI tools and clinicians both operate against.** The moment it stops being about the record primitive and starts trying to be a generic AI stack or a full EHR, the defensibility weakens sharply because there are already stronger incumbents in those adjacent categories. citeturn4view1turn8view0turn25view0turn29view0turn35view2
