# Cardiology consult — 2022-08-04

## Reason for consult
New heart failure presentation; assess and recommend management.

## HPI
64-year-old woman with longstanding hypertension and type 2 diabetes
(both diagnosed in middle age), as well as recently identified CKD
stage 3a, presenting with two weeks of worsening leg swelling and
shortness of breath on exertion. The dyspnea progressed to the point
that she could not walk to the mailbox or carry groceries from the
car. She denies chest pain, palpitations, syncope, paroxysmal
nocturnal dyspnea, or orthopnea, although she has begun sleeping
upright in a recliner over the past several days because of cough
when she lies flat. She has not previously carried a cardiac
diagnosis and has never had an echo. She denies prior IV drug use,
heavy alcohol use, or family history of cardiomyopathy. She does
have a remote 20 pack-year smoking history (quit 2012) and her
father died of an MI at 74. Compliance with her antihypertensives and
diabetes medications is good per her account and her husband's.

On admission BP was 188/102 with mild oxygen requirement and
exam findings of bibasilar crackles, elevated JVP, and 3+ pitting
edema to mid-calf bilaterally [evt-002-h001-001, evt-002-h001-002].
BNP was 612 [evt-002-h001-004]. Three sets of troponin were negative
[evt-002-h001-006]. ECG showed sinus rhythm with LVH by voltage
criteria and no ischemic changes. Today's transthoracic echocardiogram
is reviewed below.

## Echocardiogram review
- LVEF 58% by Simpson biplane; no regional wall motion abnormalities.
- Mild concentric left ventricular hypertrophy, consistent with
  longstanding hypertension.
- Grade 2 left ventricular diastolic dysfunction by E/A reversal,
  elevated E/e′ ratio of 16, and mild left atrial enlargement (LAVI
  38 mL/m²).
- Trace mitral and tricuspid regurgitation; no significant valvular
  stenosis.
- Estimated pulmonary artery systolic pressure 38 mmHg (mild
  elevation).
- IVC mildly plethoric, consistent with elevated right atrial
  pressure [evt-002-h001-014].

## Assessment
This presentation is consistent with newly diagnosed heart failure
with preserved ejection fraction (HFpEF) in the setting of:
- Longstanding hypertensive heart disease (LVH on echo, history of
  imperfectly controlled HTN, S4 on exam),
- T2DM and CKD as additional contributors to a stiff, less compliant
  left ventricle,
- A precipitant that I suspect is dietary-sodium-related plus
  imperfect adherence to her recent ACE-inhibitor swap.

Differential includes amyloid cardiomyopathy (possible but echo is
not classic — no apical sparing pattern noted, normal voltage on ECG,
no clear thickening of the septum out of proportion to free wall),
constrictive pericarditis (no pericardial features), high-output
states (none clinically), and infiltrative process (none seen). A
formal HFA-PEFF or H2FPEF score is consistent with HFpEF.

## Recommendations
1. **Diuresis**: continue IV furosemide; target net negative ~1.5–2 L
   per day until JVP normalizes, lung exam clears, and edema resolves.
   Daily standing weights, strict I&O.
2. **BP target**: SBP <130 with goal of titrating outpatient regimen
   to that target.
3. **Beta-blocker**: once euvolemic, initiate metoprolol succinate
   12.5–25 mg daily for HFpEF rate control and BP support. (Note:
   beta-blocker survival benefit in HFpEF is modest, but reasonable
   with her hypertensive component.)
4. **Continue ARB** (losartan); ARB is appropriate background therapy.
5. **Continue aspirin and atorvastatin** for ASCVD risk reduction
   (her LDL on labs in chart 4/2022 was 96, which is acceptable on
   current statin).
6. **Counsel** on a sodium-restricted diet (target <2 g/day), daily
   standing weights at home, and recognizing early HF symptoms.
7. **Outpatient cardiology follow-up** at 2 weeks; will see in clinic.
8. **Consider SGLT2 inhibitor** at outpatient follow-up given her
   T2DM and HFpEF — empagliflozin or dapagliflozin would be reasonable
   adjuncts. (Will defer at this admission given AKI.)

I have discussed the diagnosis with the patient and her husband.
She understands that HFpEF is a chronic condition that will require
ongoing management; she expressed a strong desire to remain
independent and active. They agreed to outpatient follow-up.

## Plan
Will follow daily during this admission and assume outpatient care.
