# Speech-language pathology — bedside swallow evaluation

## Reason for consult
Post-extubation bedside swallow evaluation prior to PO advancement.

## Patient status
68-year-old woman, status post 47-hour intubation for hypoxemic
respiratory failure 2/2 severe CAP and septic shock; extubated this
morning at 08:30. Currently on HFNC 30 L / 35% FiO2, alert, oriented,
following commands, conversational with mild hoarseness but no
stridor or audible airway compromise.

## Subjective
She is hungry and asks if she can eat. She denies pain with
swallowing, drooling, or cough at rest. She is at her baseline
mental status. Husband at bedside.

## Bedside swallow examination
- **Cranial nerves**: V, VII, IX, X, XII grossly intact; symmetric
  facial movement; tongue midline with full ROM; soft palate elevation
  symmetric.
- **Voice quality**: mildly hoarse, expected post-intubation; volume
  and projection adequate.
- **Oral phase trial — ice chips**: well managed, no oral residue, no
  cough.
- **Thin liquid trial — 5 mL by spoon then sips by cup**: well
  managed, no overt aspiration signs (no cough, no wet vocal quality,
  no oxygen desaturation).
- **Pureed solid trial — applesauce 5 mL**: well managed, no oral
  residue, no cough, no wet voice.
- **Soft solid trial — graham cracker bite**: chewed adequately, no
  pocketing in cheeks, swallowed in two attempts, no cough.

## Assessment
No clinical signs of aspiration on bedside swallow examination with
thin liquids and a soft/regular consistency diet. Mild post-extubation
hoarseness without stridor is expected and should improve over the
next 12–24 hours.

## Recommendations
1. Advance diet: thin liquids and soft regular diet at next meal.
2. No need for video fluoroscopic swallow study at this time.
3. Sit upright at 90 degrees during all meals; remain upright for
   30 minutes after.
4. Monitor for cough, wet voice, or fevers during PO; if any of
   these, hold PO and request repeat eval.
5. Return to consult if symptoms develop.
