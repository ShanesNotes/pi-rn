# Family meeting note — pre-intubation discussion

## Date and time
2026-04-19, 09:00–09:15.

## Location
Bedside, ICU room 8.

## Participants
- Patient (alert, distressed but able to participate)
- Husband (named healthcare power of attorney per 2022 form
  on file [enc-002-h001])
- ICU attending physician
- ICU bedside RN

## Summary of discussion
We met at the bedside to review the patient's clinical trajectory
over the past three hours. Despite high-flow nasal cannula support
to 60 L at 80%, she has had escalating work of breathing with
respiratory rate now in the high 30s, increasing accessory muscle
use, and SpO2 dropping into the high 80s. Her ABG performed at 09:00
shows pH 7.32, PaCO2 36, PaO2 56 on FiO2 0.8. This represents
hypoxemic respiratory failure that is not responding to non-invasive
support.

I explained to the patient and her husband that we recommend
proceeding with endotracheal intubation and mechanical ventilation
to support her breathing while we treat her underlying pneumonia and
septic shock. We reviewed the typical risks: hemodynamic compromise
during induction, ventilator-associated complications including
pneumonia and barotrauma, the need for sedation that will prevent
her from speaking with her family while intubated, and the small
risk of unsuccessful extubation requiring tracheostomy.

We discussed expected duration: typically 2–5 days for severe CAP
with septic shock, though the actual duration depends on her
response to antibiotics and the degree of lung injury. We also
reviewed that we will use the lightest sedation safe for her to
allow daily awakening trials, and that she may have some
recollection of tubes and procedures.

## Code status
We reviewed her code status, currently full code per her ED
discussion. The patient and her husband both confirmed they wish
to remain full code. There is no living will or POLST on file
[evt-002-h005-009].

## Healthcare power of attorney
Patient's husband is her named HCP per a form filed in chart at
the 2022 hospitalization [enc-002-h001]. He understands his role
and confirms he is comfortable making decisions on her behalf if
she becomes unable to.

## Patient and family preferences expressed
The patient, audibly anxious, said that she wants to "get better
and go home." Her husband said the same. They both expressed
strong trust in our care team. They asked that we update the
husband at every shift change and that he be called for any
significant change.

The patient's adult son and daughter (each living within an hour's
drive) were called by the husband during this conversation; the
son will arrive within the next 1–2 hours.

## Decisions
- Proceed with intubation and mechanical ventilation; husband signed
  written consent.
- Continue full code.
- Husband to be present during transitions of care; updates at each
  shift change to husband by the bedside RN.

## Plan
Intubation to occur immediately following this conversation
[evt-002-0061, evt-002-0062]. Family will be in the waiting area
during the procedure and updated immediately after.
